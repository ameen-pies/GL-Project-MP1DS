# Product

## Register

product

## Users

- **Operators**: security staff watching a live patrol map and an alert queue during a shift, often in a dim control room, sometimes on a tablet.
- **Admins**: site leads who register robots, manage access, and own the fleet inventory.
- **Security managers**: review weekly patrol coverage, battery health, and risk forecasts away from the live queue.

Every user belongs to one company. Data from other companies must never appear.

## Product Purpose

Fleet Control supervises security patrol robots: live position, battery and status, patrol missions, alerts, analytics, and an incident risk forecast. Success looks like an operator trusting the console during a shift: the map is current, an alert is impossible to miss, and every action is traceable to a person and a time.

## Brand Personality

Calm, precise, industrial. The voice is plain and operational: short labels, exact times, no hype. The interface should feel like a well-maintained control panel, not a marketing dashboard.

## Anti-references

- Generic dark-blue observability dashboards with a cyan accent.
- Neon-on-black "cyber" security theater.
- SaaS metric-card grids with giant gradient numbers.
- Glassmorphism, decorative blurs, and ornamental motion.
- Consumer-friendly rounded everything that hides operational density.

## Design Principles

1. **Honest state.** Never imply a connection, a robot, or a metric that is not real. Empty and offline states are first-class.
2. **One vocabulary.** Buttons, chips, tables, and status colors mean the same thing on every screen.
3. **The map is the workspace.** Navigation and panels orbit the live picture; they do not compete with it.
4. **Density with air.** Operators scan quickly; give data room to align, not room to decorate.
5. **Traceability.** Every action shows who did it and when, in UTC storage and local display.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA. Status is never conveyed by color alone (dot plus label). Full keyboard navigation with visible focus. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. Body text meets 4.5:1 contrast; large text and UI borders meet 3:1.
